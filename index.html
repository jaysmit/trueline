The problem

Six months of spending is not six months of living.

A statement records what left the account. It has no way of saying which of it will still be happening next year — and that distinction is the entire assessment.

Counted, but never again

The one-off purchase

Moving costs, a wedding, a replacement fridge, a car repair. It lands once in the window and a monthly average treats it as a permanent commitment.

Counted, but not spending

The money that never left

Transfers to a savings account, an offset, a partner or a second account of their own. It looks identical to an expense and it isn't one.

Counted at the wrong size

The reimbursed and the shared

Fuel and tolls an employer pays back. A dinner one person paid for and everyone split. The debit is real; the household's cost isn't.

Every one of these needs the same thing to resolve: a reason. The reason isn't on the statement — it's with the person who spent the money. Until now, getting it meant an email chain, one transaction at a time, with the answers scattered across a mailbox and nothing left on the file to show for it.

What it costs the broker today

  • One email per questionThen waiting. Then a reminder. Then a reply that answers half of it.
  • Answers scattered across a mailboxThree months later you cannot find the message that justified the number.
  • A file note written from memoryReconstructed after the fact, because nothing recorded the reasoning as it happened.
What it costs the borrower today
  • Questions with no context“What was this $4,200?” — with no idea why it matters or what turns on the answer.
  • Handing over everythingFull statements by email, names and account numbers included, with no idea where they go.
  • No way to raise anythingThey know the wedding was a one-off. Nobody asked, so it stays in the figure.
  • Silence at the endThey never find out what was accepted, what was not, or why.

The solution

An afternoon of email, in about ten minutes.

The same questions get asked and the same judgement gets made. What changes is that they happen once, in the right place, with the answers already attached to the transaction.

For the broker~10 min review
  • One request, not twenty emails. Every question goes out together and every answer comes back attached to the line it explains.
  • Ranked by what it’s worth. The transactions that move the figure sit at the top, with the monthly impact on each, so you work down and stop when it stops mattering.
  • Nothing moves without you. A borrower’s explanation is a proposal. You set the final amount, approve, reduce or decline.
  • The file note writes itself. Both notes, the evidence and a timestamp on every adjustment — including the ones you refused.
For the borrower~10 min to answer
  • One link, no account. They open it on their phone, upload their statements, and never install anything or set a password.
  • They can see why they’re being asked. Each question shows what the transaction is worth per month, so they know which ones actually matter.
  • They answer in their own words. Plain language and a photo of a receipt, not a spreadsheet or a category dropdown.
  • They find out where it landed. Approved, reduced or declined, with the reason — instead of silence and a number they never see.

What the borrower confirms

Ask once. On the transaction. In their own words.

The borrower sees the transactions that need a reason, ordered by what each one is worth per month. They answer where the transaction lives and attach evidence if they have it. Everything stays editable — this is an ordering, not a gate.

They see what it's worth

A borrower who can see that a question is worth $520 a month answers it properly, and answers it first.

An explanation is a proposal, not a change

Nothing moves the figure until you act on it. You can accept it, decline it, or substitute your own number and say why.

The answer stays on the transaction

Not in an inbox. When you come back to the file in three months, the reason is still sitting next to the line it explains.

The documentation

A reason on its own isn't enough. It has to be on the file.

The transaction is never edited away. The amount, date and description stay exactly as the bank recorded them. What changes is how the assessment treats them — and every change carries who made it, when, and what they relied on.

Evidence travels with the transaction

The borrower's wording and anything they attach stay on the line they explain, not in a mailbox you'd have to go back through.

Declining is recorded too

A proposal you rejected, and the number you substituted instead, is part of the record. That's often the entry that matters most.
